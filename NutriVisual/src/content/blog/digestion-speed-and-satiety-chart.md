How long food stays in your stomach (**gastric emptying time**) and how full you feel after eating (**satiety index**) dictate appetite regulation, blood sugar stability, and energy levels.

Digestion speed varies drastically by macronutrient composition:
- **Simple Liquids & Sugars:** 15–30 minutes
- **Complex Carbohydrates:** 1–2 hours
- **Lean Proteins:** 2–3 hours
- **Dense Fats & Whole Meats:** 4–6+ hours

Below is a science-backed reference breakdown comparing digestion speed in hours alongside official satiety index scores.

---

## The Science of Gastric Emptying

Gastric emptying is controlled by the stomach's pyloric sphincter. Liquids pass through rapidly, while solid foods must be ground into chyme particles smaller than 2mm before entering the duodenum.

Fat content triggers the gut hormone **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** and **GLP-1**, which actively slow down stomach motility to ensure proper bile emulsification and nutrient absorption.

## 3 Pillars of High Satiety Meal Building

1. **Volume & Water Weight (Stomach Stretch):** High-volume foods like potatoes, oranges, and green vegetables trigger mechanoreceptor stretch receptors without adding excessive calories.
2. **Protein Density (Satiety Signaling):** Protein triggers PYY (Peptide YY) and GLP-1 release, directly signalling satiety to the hypothalamus.
3. **Viscous Soluble Fiber (Delayed Emptying):** Oats, chia seeds, and legumes form a viscous gel in the digestive tract, slowing carbohydrate absorption and blunting postprandial glucose spikes.

## How to Optimize Your Meal Timing

- **Pre-Workout Fuel:** Opt for fast-digesting carbohydrates (rice, fruit, oats) 60–90 minutes before exercise for immediate glycogen availability without gastric distress.
- **Evening Meals for Overnight Recovery:** Pair slow-digesting proteins (casein, beef, salmon) with healthy fats to sustain amino acid elevation during overnight sleep.
