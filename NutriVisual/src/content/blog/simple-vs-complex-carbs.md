## Glycemic Load and the Insulin Response

Carbohydrates are the body's primary source of glucose, but how they enter your bloodstream determines your metabolic health. 

Simple carbohydrates (processed grains, sugars) lack a fiber buffer. They are rapidly converted into glucose, causing a steep spike in blood sugar. In response, your pancreas secretes a surge of insulin to clear glucose from the blood. This rapid clearance triggers a "sugar crash," leaving you fatigued, irritable, and craving more quick energy within 90 minutes.

---

## The Fiber Matrix of Complex Carbs

Complex carbohydrates like quinoa and sweet potatoes are structurally bound to insoluble and soluble fibers. This fiber matrix creates a physical gel in the stomach, slowing gastric emptying and glucose absorption.

| Carbohydrate Type | Glycemic Index | Energy Curve | Gut Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple (White Bread)** | High (75) | Rapid Spike & Crash | Minimal microbiome fuel |
| **Complex (Quinoa)** | Low (53) | Flat, sustained release | High prebiotic fermentation |

Choosing complex carbohydrates keeps insulin levels stable, protecting pancreatic function and ensuring hours of steady, focused brain energy.
