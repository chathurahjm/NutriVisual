## What Are the Lowest Calorie Foods That Fill You Up?

The lowest calorie foods that maximize fullness are **non-starchy vegetables with high water content and fiber**. The top three are **kimchi (15 kcal/100g)**, **zucchini (17 kcal/100g)**, and **spinach (23 kcal/100g)** — allowing you to eat enormous volumes for almost zero caloric cost.

## The Science: Why Volume Matters More Than Calories

Your stomach doesn't count calories — it measures **physical volume**. Mechanoreceptors (stretch receptors) in your stomach wall trigger satiety signals through the vagus nerve when your stomach physically expands.

This means:
- **11g of olive oil** (100 kcal) = barely registers in your stomach
- **588g of zucchini** (100 kcal) = completely fills your stomach

Both contain the exact same number of calories, but the zucchini activates maximum satiety signaling while the olive oil activates none.

## How to Use Low-Calorie Foods Strategically

### The Volumetric Pre-Load Strategy
Research from Penn State University shows that eating a **large salad or broth-based soup** before your main meal reduces total meal calorie intake by **12–20%** without reducing satisfaction.

**Pre-load template:**
1. Start with 200–300g of raw vegetables (spinach, bell pepper, carrots) = ~50 kcal
2. Add bone broth or miso soup = ~45 kcal
3. Then eat your normal meal

Total pre-load cost: ~95 calories, but you'll eat 150–300 fewer calories in your main course.

### The Plate Composition Rule
Fill your plate using this ratio:
- **50% non-starchy vegetables** (broccoli, spinach, zucchini)
- **25% lean protein** (chicken breast, fish, tofu)
- **25% complex carbohydrates** (sweet potato, quinoa, lentils)

This naturally caps calorie density at ~100–150 kcal per 100g of plated food while providing maximum nutrient diversity.
