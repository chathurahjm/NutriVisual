## What Happens If You Eat Too Much Protein?

Consuming protein beyond your body's utilization capacity (generally above 2.2–3.0 g/kg/day for extended periods) can lead to digestive discomfort, dehydration, and nutrient displacement — though the risks are often overstated for healthy individuals.

---

## Short-Term Effects of Excess Protein

| Symptom | Why It Happens | Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Digestive bloating & gas** | Undigested protein ferments in the colon, producing hydrogen sulfide gas | Varies by individual |
| **Dehydration** | Protein metabolism produces urea, requiring water for kidney excretion | >2.0 g/kg/day without adequate hydration |
| **Bad breath (ketosis-like)** | Ammonia byproduct of amino acid deamination | High protein + very low carb diets |
| **Constipation** | High protein diets often displace fiber-rich foods | When fiber drops below 25g/day |

---

## Is Too Much Protein Bad for Your Kidneys?

**For healthy individuals: No.** A 2018 meta-analysis in the *Journal of Nutrition* found no evidence that high-protein diets (up to 2.2 g/kg/day) cause kidney damage in people with normal renal function.

**For those with pre-existing kidney disease: Yes, it can accelerate decline.** Excess protein increases glomerular filtration rate (GFR), which is harmless for healthy kidneys but strains compromised ones.

---

## The Real Risk: Nutrient Displacement

The most common problem with very high protein diets is not protein toxicity — it's what protein *replaces* in your diet:

- **Displaces fiber**: Reduced intake of fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
- **Displaces micronutrients**: Less variety means fewer vitamins and minerals
- **Increases saturated fat** (if protein comes primarily from red meat and dairy)

## How Much Protein Is Actually Too Much?

For most healthy adults, the practical upper limit is around **3.0 g/kg/day** based on available research. Beyond this:
- No additional muscle-building benefit
- Increased caloric load without proportional benefit
- Potential digestive discomfort

For a 70kg (154 lb) person, this translates to roughly **210g of protein per day** — equivalent to eating about 680g (1.5 lbs) of chicken breast daily.

---

## The Bottom Line

For the vast majority of people, the real problem is eating **too little** protein, not too much. Protein deficiency is far more common and far more damaging than excess protein intake. Focus on hitting 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day from diverse sources, staying well hydrated, and maintaining adequate fiber intake.
