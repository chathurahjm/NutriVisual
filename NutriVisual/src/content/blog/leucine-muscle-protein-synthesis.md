## Biochemical Driver of Muscle Protein Synthesis

In exercise physiology and sports nutrition, **Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)** is the fundamental biological process responsible for muscle tissue repair, hyper-trophic adaptation, and the mitigation of age-related sarcopenia.

While all nine essential amino acids (EAAs) are required to construct new muscle proteins, a single branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) acts as the primary molecular trigger: **L-Leucine**.

---

## The mTORC1 Pathway & The Leucine Trigger

Leucine functions not merely as a structural building block for muscle protein, but as a direct **nutrient sensor and signaling molecule**.

### 1. Sestrin2 & GATOR Signaling
Inside skeletal muscle cells, leucine binds directly to an intracellular amino acid sensor protein called **Sestrin2**. 

When intracellular leucine levels reach a critical threshold, Sestrin2 disassociates from the **GATOR2 complex**, allowing GATOR2 to activate **mTORC1** (mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1)—the master regulator of cell growth and protein translation.

### 2. Upstream Kinase Phosphorylation
Once mTORC1 is activated, it phosphorylates downstream effector proteins:
* **p70S6K (Ribosomal Protein S6 Kinase 1):** Accelerates mRNA translation efficiency.
* **4E-BP1 (Eukaryotic Translation Initiation Factor 4E-Binding Protein 1):** Releases initiation factor eIF4E, triggering de novo protein assembly.

---

## The "Leucine Threshold" Concept

Clinical isotope tracer studies demonstrate that a minimum concentration of leucine must be absorbed rapidly into portal circulation to fully stimulate maximal MPS. This is known as the **Leucine Threshold**:

* **Young Adults (~20–35 yrs):** Require approximately **2.0g to 3.0g of leucine** per meal (~0.05g/kg body weight).
* **Older Adults (50+ yrs):** Due to "anabolic resistance" (decreased sensitivity of Sestrin2/mTORC1), older individuals require **3.5g to 4.5g of leucine** per meal to achieve equivalent MPS stimulation.

## DIAAS & PDCAAS Protein Quality Standards

When evaluating protein sources for leucine density and muscle retention, modern nutrition science relies on the **Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score (DIAAS)** rather than the older PDCAAS scale.

DIAAS measures ileal amino acid digestibility at the end of the small intestine. Animal proteins (eggs, dairy, poultry, beef) typically score above 1.0 to 1.3 DIAAS, indicating superior leucine bioavailability. Plant proteins (wheat, legumes) often have lower DIAAS scores (0.6 to 0.9) due to lower leucine concentration and anti-nutritional factors (such as phytates), requiring slightly larger serving sizes to hit the 3g leucine threshold.

## Practical Action Steps for Anabolic Optimization

1. **Space Protein Meals 3–5 Hours Apart:** MPS remains elevated for 2 to 3 hours after reaching the leucine threshold. Spacing meals allows intracellular leucine levels to reset.
2. **Hit 3g Leucine per Meal:** Ensure each main meal contains 25g–40g of high-quality protein to guarantee crossing the leucine threshold.
3. **Supplement Plant-Based Meals:** If consuming plant-based proteins (such as tofu or beans), increase total protein intake per meal by 20–30% or fortify with extra leucine to overcome anabolic resistance.
