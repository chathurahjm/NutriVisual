## The Battle of the Dairy Fats

Healthy fats have reclaimed their rightful place at the center of the longevity conversation. Among biohackers and health enthusiasts, grass-fed butter and grass-fed ghee (clarified butter) are two of the most popular sources of lipids. But which one actually delivers a superior biochemical payout?

To understand the difference, we have to look at how they are made. Butter is composed of milk fat, water, and milk solids (proteins and sugars like casein and lactose). Ghee is created by heating butter to evaporate the water and skimming off the caramelized milk solids, leaving behind pure liquid gold fat.

## Smoke Points and Cooking Chemistry

One of the biggest practical differences between the two lies in their smoke points. Because ghee has had all its milk solids removed, its smoke point increases significantly to 485°F (250°C), compared to butter's smoke point of just 350°F (177°C).

When you cook past a fat's smoke point, the fat oxidizes, releasing harmful free radicals and acrolein. For high-heat cooking, searing, or roasting, ghee is the clear winner for protecting your cellular health from inflammatory oxidized oils.

## Gut Health and Tolerability

Ghee is also naturally free of lactose and casein. If you have a dairy sensitivity or digestive issues, swapping butter for ghee can eliminate bloating and systemic inflammation.

Furthermore, ghee contains a slightly higher concentration of butyric acid—a short-chain fatty acid that serves as the primary fuel source for your gut's colonocytes. Supporting your gut lining is essential for maintaining strong immune health and preventing leaky gut.
