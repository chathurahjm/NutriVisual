## The Gel Buffer vs. The Intestinal Sweep

Fiber is a non-digestible carbohydrate, but it is one of the most critical elements of metabolic and gut longevity. Fiber is categorized into two forms:

1. **Soluble Fiber**: Dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance in your digestive tract. This gel slows down gastric emptying, delaying glucose absorption and preventing blood sugar spikes.
2. **Insoluble Fiber**: Does not dissolve in water. It adds bulk to stool, accelerating transit time and supporting bowel motility.

---

## SCFA Generation and Gut Transit

Both fibers are fermented by your gut bacteria to produce **Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs)** like butyrate, which fuel the gut lining and regulate inflammation.

| Feature | Soluble Fiber | Insoluble Fiber |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Forms gel, slows digestion | Adds bulk, speeds transit |
| **Key Sources** | Chia Seeds, Flaxseed, Oats | Whole grains, leafy greens |
| **Metabolic Benefit** | Stabilizes insulin response | Promotes bowel motility |

Consuming seeds like chia and flax provides a rich ratio of both soluble and insoluble fibers, buffering metabolic load.
