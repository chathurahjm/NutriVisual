## Soluble Fiber: The Blood Sugar Buffer

Soluble fiber dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance in your digestive tract. This gel slows down the digestion of starch and simple sugars, preventing rapid insulin spikes and promoting stable energy.

Foods rich in soluble fiber include Chia Seeds, Flaxseed, and Lentils. They actively bind to bile acids, helping to lower LDL cholesterol levels.

## Insoluble Fiber: The Microbiome Scaffold

Insoluble fiber does not dissolve in water. It acts as a sweep in your intestines, supporting bowel regularity and physical motility.

Moreover, both types of fiber undergo fermentation by your gut bacteria to produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) like acetate, propionate, and butyrate, which act as metabolic signaling molecules to reduce systemic inflammation.
