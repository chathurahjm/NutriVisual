## Why Protein-Per-Calorie Matters

When optimizing for body composition — whether building muscle or losing fat — **total calories still matter**. Two foods can both contain 25g of protein, but one might cost you 130 calories (tuna) while the other costs 654 calories (walnuts).

### The Protein Leverage Hypothesis

Research from the University of Sydney demonstrates that humans have a fixed protein appetite. If your meals are low in protein density, your brain drives you to keep eating until you hit your protein target — leading to overconsumption of fats and carbohydrates in the process.

Choosing high protein-per-calorie foods means you:
- **Hit your protein target faster** with fewer total calories
- **Experience greater satiety** per meal
- **Maintain muscle mass** during caloric deficit

## Common Protein Myths

### "You can only absorb 30g of protein per meal"
**False.** Your body absorbs all protein you consume. The 30g figure refers to the *maximal rate of muscle protein synthesis stimulation per meal* — but excess protein is still utilized for tissue repair, enzyme production, and energy.

### "Plant protein is incomplete and inferior"
**Partially true, but misleading.** While most individual plant proteins lack one or more essential amino acids, combining sources (e.g., rice + beans, tofu + quinoa) easily provides a complete amino acid profile. Soy-based proteins (tofu, tempeh, edamame) are already complete on their own.
